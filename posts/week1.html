The Marvel Manhunt

A hero is hiding somewhere in Wikipedia's Category:Marvel Comics superheroes link network. You can't see them — but every character you question can tell you exactly how many wiki-links stand between the two of them. Can you triangulate the target?

Research question

Wikipedia's internal links between the 303 Marvel superhero articles form a small, dense social-style network. If we treat "how many clicks apart are two characters" as a distance, is that distance informative enough for a human to home in on a hidden target purely from hot/cold feedback — the way you'd play a real-life game of Where's Waldo? And along the way: who are the hubs everyone points to, who are the over-eager linkers nobody links back, and what does the network's structure (giant component, isolated nodes, disconnected cliques) actually look like?

Method, in one paragraph

The raw data is directed: an edge A → B means the Wikipedia article for A links to B, and links are rarely reciprocated (see below). For a distance that behaves sensibly — finite, symmetric, "closer is closer" — the manhunt below collapses the graph to undirected edges and measures distance with breadth-first search. The target is always drawn from the network's giant component, since a distance to an isolated node is either 0 or infinite and makes for a very short game.

          Discussion

          The manhunt game turns a somewhat dry methodological choice — symmetrizing a directed graph so that BFS distance is well-defined — into something you can actually feel: because the average shortest path is short (see the diameter stat above), a handful of good guesses almost always close the case. That's the small-world property of this network made tangible. The one place the game intentionally breaks is the 17 isolates and the 9-character clique above: structurally, no amount of "hot/cold" reasoning can ever reach them from the rest of the universe, which is itself a finding worth sitting with.
